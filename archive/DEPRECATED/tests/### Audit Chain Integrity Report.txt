### Purpose
Document the integrity verification performed on the Mythara Engine audit chain (audit logs, audit_chain entries, signed manifests, and related checksums). Confirm tamper-evidence, signature validity, and traceability of substitutions and sanitizer events referenced in validation artifacts.

---

### Scope
- Files and artifacts verified:
  - manifest/RELEASE_MANIFEST.json  
  - manifest/checksums.sha256 and manifest/checksums.sha256.asc  
  - signer_public_key.asc  
  - verification_report.txt  
  - audit logs: audit_chain/*.log (structured JSON entries)  
  - sanitizer_events.csv, leakage_probe_log.csv, explainability_ratings.csv, determinism_results/*  
- Operations performed:
  - PGP signature verification of checksums file  
  - SHA-256 checksum validation for all listed artifacts  
  - Structural and schema validation of audit_chain entries  
  - Cross-referencing of audit entries to incident_ids, sanitizer events, and manifest artifacts  
  - Tamper-detection via append-only checks and signed action records

---

### Methods and tools used
- GnuPG for detached signature verification (gpg --verify) using signer_public_key.asc.  
- sha256sum for checksum verification (sha256sum -c manifest/checksums.sha256).  
- JSON schema validation for audit_chain entries (required fields: action_type, actor_id, artifact_path, manifest_ref, prompt_hash, rule_id, timestamp_utc, signature_hash).  
- Append-only audit verification: ensure each audit record contains previous_record_hash and that the chain of previous_record_hash values links to the genesis record.  
- Recompute per-record hashes (SHA-256 of canonicalized JSON) and compare to stored signature_hash values.  
- Cross-link validation: ensure incident_id references in audit entries map to files in tests/ or to rows in sanitizer_events.csv or leakage_probe_log.csv.

- All files listed in manifest/checksums.sha256 returned OK under sha256sum -c, except for the following that required revalidation after documented remediation:
  - core/renderer.py (hash updated from v1.7.2 to v1.7.3) — reconciled with updated manifest and new checksum entry (present).  
  - tests/determinism_results/det-incident-0427/run_2_output.json — content changed during remediation; manifest lists post-remediation canonical outputs and checksums; historical pre-patch outputs archived under tests/determinism_results_archive/ with separate checksum set.  
- signer_public_key.asc included and matches the key used to sign manifest/checksums.sha256.

### Findings — audit chain structural integrity
- Total audit_chain records inspected: 12,432 entries (audit_chain/*.log).  
- Schema compliance: 100% of sampled entries (12,432/12,432) include required fields and valid ISO-8601 UTC timestamps.  
- Append-only linkage: every record contains previous_record_hash; recomputed hashes match stored previous_record_hash links except for one sequence anomaly (see Anomalies).  
- Per-record signature_hash validation: 12,431/12,432 records matched recomputed SHA-256 of canonicalized JSON content. One record (audit id AC-9832) had a mismatched signature_hash; investigation follows.

### Cross-reference validation
- All incident_ids referenced in audit_chain entries map to incident files in tests/ or rows in sanitizer_events.csv/leakage_probe_log.csv/explainability_ratings.csv, except for three legacy references that point to archived artifacts in tests/determinism_results_archive/ (intentionally preserved for forensic history).  
- Sanitizer and leakage events cited by audit entries correspond to rows in sanitizer_events.csv and leakage_probe_log.csv with matching timestamps and event_ids.  
- Substitution actions reference fallback_clause_catalog entries and manifest clause IDs; referenced clause hashes match current manifest entries or are present in redaction_log for archived variations.

### Anomalies and remediation
1. Sequence anomaly: audit_chain segment between 2025-10-12T21:03:05Z and 2025-10-12T21:04:12Z showed an out-of-order previous_record_hash link due to an agent-side ingestion retry that created a duplicate record and appended entries with a duplicated pointer. Impact: audit lineage remains reconstructable; remediation: duplicate record AC-4921 marked and retained for forensic trace; agent ingestion process patched to enforce strict monotonic sequence numbers (fixed and redeployed 2025-10-13T03:00Z).  
2. Mismatched signature_hash: record AC-9832 (timestamp 2025-10-29T09:16:05Z) had a stored signature_hash that did not match recomputed hash of its canonicalized payload. Root cause: post-write reformatting of non-essential whitespace in the audit logger changed canonicalization before final hashing in a rare race condition. Remediation: mark AC-9832 as "integrity-warning"; an independent duplicate of the original canonical payload was recovered from agent local spool and its hash matches stored signature_hash; audit log entry AC-9832 was reissued as AC-9832-R with an explicit note and preserved evidence; logger patched to write canonicalized payload atomically (fix deployed 2025-10-29T14:22Z).  
3. Historical artifact update: core/renderer.py was updated (v1.7.3) and canonical outputs rechecked; manifest and checksums updated accordingly. All changes recorded in verification_report.txt and linked from audit_chain with change action entries that include patch diff hashes. No unauthorized modification detected.

All anomalies are documented, time-stamped, and linked to remediation commits and verification artifacts in verification_report.txt.

---

### Tamper-evidence assessment
- No unauthorized modifications found: every change to artifacts observed in manifest/checksums.sha256 is associated with a signed commit, a verification_report entry, or a documented remediation action.  
- The single integrity-warning (AC-9832) has preserved forensic evidence (agent spool, archived copy) that proves the recorded action is authentic and was subject to a logging race condition rather than external tampering.  
- Audit chain append-only property remains enforceable; agent-side ingestion hardening reduces future risk of sequence anomalies.

---

### Recommendations
- Enforce canonical JSON serialization (sorted keys, no insignificant whitespace) at write-time for all audit records and compute signature_hash before any further processing or persistence.  
- Integrate audit_chain validation into nightly CI and require zero anomalies as a precondition for weekly manifest releases.  
- Retain duplicate signed archival copies of critical audit sequences (e.g., substitution and sanitizer clusters) in a write-once storage to aid future forensic needs.  
- Automate cross-reference checks between audit_chain, sanitizer_events.csv, and leakage_probe_log.csv as part of release verification.  
- Consider hardware-backed key signing (HSM or YubiKey) for manifest/checksums.sha256 to reduce risk of key compromise and provide stronger non-repudiation.

---

### Conclusion
The Mythara Engine audit chain shows strong integrity, with valid signatures and checksums for current canonical artifacts. Detected anomalies were minor, documented, and remediated with traceable commits and verification artifacts. The audit chain remains reliable for auditor use and for escrowed reproducibility verification, subject to implementing the recommended hardening steps.
